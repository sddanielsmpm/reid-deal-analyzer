What We Saw

Observed from live page text and fetched HTML source for both experiences.

Onsite Get Matched reinvestorguide.com

  • Page promise: "Find Your Perfect Investor Loan" with free lender matching copy.
  • Form title: "Get Matched With a Lender".
  • Four manual steps: Loan Type, Property, Details, Contact.
  • Starts with 11 loan type options, including "Not Sure".
  • Property step asks ZIP code and property type.
  • Details step asks loan amount, credit score, investor experience, and bankruptcy.
  • Contact step asks name, email, phone, and TCPA consent.
  • Trust blocks appear after the form: 100% Free, No Obligation, Compare Lenders.

Third-party benchmark mpm.secure-clix.com

  • Headline promise: "Explore Your Mortgage Options".
  • Starts with a simpler intent question: purchase, refinance, or cash out.
  • Uses one-question screens with many auto-advance choice fields.
  • Asks user identity early: homeowner, self-employed, investor, broker, or other.
  • Asks property value before loan amount to frame the request.
  • Includes timing, occupancy, loan position, realtor, military, credit, and contact steps.
  • Repeats "private, safe and secure" microcopy near sensitive inputs.
  • End-of-form reassurance says no login or SSN, no credit impact, under 1 minute, and information will not be sold to multiple parties.
  • Includes LeadiD and TrustedForm scripts for consent certification.

Conversion Moves To Borrow

The benchmark's strongest patterns are mostly transferable without copying the entire generic mortgage flow.

Momentum

Start with intent, not jargon

A first-time visitor may not know whether they need DSCR, bridge, hard money, or bank statement financing. Lead with "What are you trying to finance?" and route to loan type behind the scenes.

Chunking

One decision per screen

The onsite form compresses several underwriting-style fields into a few screens. The benchmark slows the perceived ask while speeding the interaction through single-choice screens.

Reassurance

Move trust into the form

The onsite trust blocks are useful, but they sit after the form. Put privacy, no SSN, no credit impact, and no obligation language at the exact moments users hesitate.

Personalization

Use answers to tailor the next prompt

Investor-specific routing can make the onsite form feel smarter than the benchmark: rental purchase, fix-and-flip, refinance, bridge, and "not sure" can each get contextual copy.

Proof

Explain what happens next

Replace generic submit energy with a concrete result: "Show my lender matches" or "Match me with investor-friendly lenders in my market."

Compliance

Certify consent cleanly

The benchmark has LeadiD and TrustedForm. If partner economics depend on verifiable consent, add equivalent certification to the onsite funnel before scaling paid traffic.

Proposed V1 Flow

A guided investor quiz that captures the same business-critical fields while improving early momentum and trust.

Core hypothesis

If we replace the loan-type-first form with a guided investor financing quiz, then more users will start and complete because the first actions are easier, the experience feels personalized, and high-friction questions are delayed until intent is established.

1 Financing goal Rental purchase, refinance/cash out, fix-and-flip, bridge, construction, not sure.
2 Investor profile First investment, active investor, self-employed, broker/agent, non-US investor, other.
3 Property type Single family, 2-4 units, 5+ units, condo/townhome, mixed-use, commercial, land.
4 Location Property ZIP or "still shopping" current ZIP. Keep address optional until needed.
5 Property value Use buckets first. It helps users answer faster and creates LTV context.
6 Loan amount Currency input with "estimate is fine" copy and simple formatting.
7 Timing ASAP, 2-4 weeks, 1-3 months, researching. Useful for routing and sales priority.
8 Credit range Ask after value is built. Add "will not affect credit" next to this question.
9 Contact Name, email, phone, TCPA consent. Lead with "where should we send your matches?"
10 Post-submit route Show next-step confirmation, expected timing, and lender match context.

Measurement Plan

Use the existing event taxonomy and add enough dimensions to diagnose where each variant wins or loses.

Metric or event Definition Required properties
Primary metric Completed qualified leads divided by unique form starts. form_id, variant_id, traffic_source, device_type, qualification_status
Secondary metric Completed leads divided by landing page sessions. page_type, offer, session_id, variant_id, referrer
form_start First interaction with the form, not just page view. form_id, variant_id, first_step_id, traffic_source
form_step_view Each step displayed, including repeated visits after back navigation. step_number, step_id, field_group, variant_id
form_field_error Validation error, blocked advance, invalid email, invalid phone, or ZIP lookup fail. field_name, error_type, step_number, value_present
